    BEFORE ME, MOON / 03

    Some people inherit a home. Others spend their whole lives trying to remember the way back.

    Before Me, Moon moves through hospital rooms, empty streets, addiction, childhood, love, memory, freedom and the strange places a mind can go when nobody else is awake.

    Written from notebooks rather than explanations, these poems do not ask to be understood perfectly.

    They ask to be entered.

    • There are doors that keep moving.
    • Rooms that remember bodies.
    • Files that remember older versions of people.

    And somewhere above all of it, the moon keeps watching without asking for proof.

    This is not a book about having survived everything.

    It is about still being here when the page turns.

    BEFORE ME, MOON / 04

    A Note Before You Read

    I didn’t write these poems to explain myself.

    Most of them were written before I fully understood what they meant. Some are memories. Some are things that happened. Some are distortions, daydreams, fears, places, people, or feelings I could only understand after putting them on paper.

    Not every line is literal.

    Not every metaphor belongs to only one thing.

    The moon keeps returning because some things can witness you without interrupting you.

    I don’t believe a poem has one correct meaning. I know what some of these words mean to me, but once you read them, they might start meaning something completely different to you.

    That doesn’t make either of us wrong.

    Sometimes the page knows before the writer does.

    — Mike Ramírez

    Efface

    The streets turned cimmerian before
I was ready to go home.

Vesper settled over the rooftop before
anyone noticed. The rain felt lustral but
nothing in me came clean.

I was walking in the park and kept
meeting an epidolon of myself in old
pictures I see in my head. I wanted
something amaranthine in a life built to
disappear.

My thoughts took an anfractuous route
back to the same wound that isn’t healed
yet. I keep searching for the quiddity of
home in rooms I am leaving. The morning felt
ineluctable, even when I wanted the nights to
stay. Your name still touches cadence my
mouth remembers. Time tried to efface me
but forgot the corners.
— M.R

    Verdict Name

    The walls still know my name, But the
people have forgotten the letters of
my stay, I wonder if this is A mezzanine.

They gave me A deadline, But never told
me when the waiting ends. Like A unfinished
poem searching it's place in A world i
cannot name.

It took me years to learn that A door could
stay open. Now I knock Before entering my
own Life. Like A expiration date in
transparent ink, evidence without sentence
I could not even speak. Like my name it's
meaning is A verdict instead of letters in
A random order creating A soul.
— M.R

    The book — out now

    Some books tell you a story.

    Memory, identity, love, loss and freedom — set down personally, unfiltered, and sometimes uncomfortably close.

    There is no single correct way to read them. A line may come to mean something to you that it never meant to the person who wrote it.

    BEFORE ME, MOON / 09

    To the person holding this book

    Someone once told me I shouldn’t put all of my poems online.

    “That’s too good to read for free.”

    I laughed, but I kept the sentence.

    So if you bought this book, borrowed it, shared a page with someone, waited for it, supported it, or simply made it this far:

    thank you.

    A book only needs one person to write it.

    But it needs someone else to become real.
